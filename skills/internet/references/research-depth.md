# Research Depth

Use depth as a contract with the user. More depth means more iterations, broader source coverage, and more explicit uncertainty handling.

## Quick

Use for straightforward fact checks, simple current lookups, and questions where the user clearly wants speed.

- Search passes: 1-2.
- Sources opened: 3-5.
- Minimum source mix: one primary or authoritative source when available, plus one independent source for corroboration.
- Output: direct answer with citations and one caveat sentence if needed.

## Standard

Use by default for research requests.

- Search passes: 3-4.
- Sources opened: 8-15.
- Minimum source mix: primary source, independent reporting or expert source, and a contradiction or risk search.
- Output: findings, evidence, confidence, caveats, and recommendation.

## Deep

Use when the user asks for detailed, exhaustive, serious, report-grade, due diligence, "anything required", or high-stakes research.

- Search passes: 5-8.
- Sources opened: 20+ when available.
- Minimum source mix: primary sources, independent sources, community or ground-truth sources, contradiction searches, and archive or historical sources when relevant.
- Output: executive summary, methodology, key findings, evidence table or claim ledger, conflicts, gaps, and next actions.

## Exhaustive

Use only when explicitly requested or when the decision is high-cost and the user expects a research artifact.

- Search passes: continue until saturation.
- Sources opened: as many as needed, grouped by source class.
- Add source inventory with inclusion and exclusion notes.
- Keep a query ledger and explain what was not accessible.

## Saturation Test

Stop when three consecutive targeted searches produce no materially new evidence, no new source class, and no meaningful contradiction.

Do one final gap pass before synthesis:

- Search for the strongest counterclaim.
- Search for the newest update.
- Search official source plus exact claim terms.
- Search date-bounded queries when the timeline matters.

## Escalation Triggers

Move from Quick to Standard when:

- The user asks "latest", "best", "compare", "should I", "is this true", or "what changed".
- The result could change spending, travel, health, legal, financial, security, or business decisions.
- Sources disagree.

Move from Standard to Deep when:

- The prompt is broad or ambiguous.
- The answer depends on multiple entities.
- The source trail crosses web, documents, social, code, videos, and official records.
- The user asks for a report, strategy, market map, due diligence, competitive analysis, or investigation.
